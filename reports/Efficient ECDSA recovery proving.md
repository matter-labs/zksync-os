# Witnessed keys and block batching beat recomputed ecrecover

Yes, two techniques prove secp256k1 recovery with fewer group operations than recomputing `u1·G + u2·R`, and both work by witnessing the recovered key `Q` instead of deriving it: a 4-dimensional lattice decomposition (GLV composed with a half-GCD in the Eisenstein integers, the "fake GLV" of El Housni and Eagen) rewrites the verification equation so every variable-base scalar is ≈64 bits, cutting the chain from ≈128 to ≈64 doublings and from ≈190–203 to ≈147–162 group operations (**20–28% fewer hinted divisions and field multiplications**; the measured in-circuit analogue is −26.8% for one secp256k1 scalar multiplication); and a random-linear-combination batch over all recoveries in a block, evaluated as one Pippenger MSM, brings the amortised cost to **≈96 group operations per signature at n = 100 and ≈56 at n = 1,000**, a 2–3.5× reduction, at the price of making every recovered address a hint until block end and of a Fiat–Shamir transcript with 128-bit coefficients. Everything else surveyed is exhausted or inapplicable: every deployed zkVM and zkEVM hints exactly the same four field operations you already hint (inverse, division/slope, square root, quotient), none verifies a scalar multiple cheaper than it computes it, hinted affine arithmetic with the fused `2R+P` step is already 3–4× cheaper per operation than any inversion-free coordinate system, wider generator tables buy under 5%, ECIP costs ≈780 modular multiplications per point in its only native implementation, and secp256k1 admits no pairing, no Montgomery or Edwards model and no useful curve cycle for a RISC-V guest whose field arithmetic is already delegated. One further finding reframes the cycle budget: at **27 cycles and 6.25 delegation calls per field multiplication**, the ≈600 multiplication-equivalents of a recovery explain only ≈16k of the 66k cycles, so non-arithmetic overhead per group operation is the larger pool and shrinks in proportion to whichever of the two techniques you adopt.

## Every deployed prover hints the same four field operations and still pays per group operation

The hint model has converged. SP1's k256 patch calls host hooks for the field inverse and square root and checks the answers with one multiplication and a canonicality test, halting with a dedicated exit code 3 on a bad hint so that "a malicious prover cannot forge a panicked-program proof" ([sp1-patches field.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sp1-patches/elliptic-curves/patch-k256-13.4-sp1-6.10.0/k256/src/arithmetic/field.rs); [sp1-lib invalid_hint](https://docs.rs/sp1-lib/latest/sp1_lib/macro.invalid_hint.html)). RISC Zero's `bigint2` asserts `result < modulus` on every checked op because "a dishonest prover can sometimes return a result greater than the prime" ([risc0-bigint2](https://docs.rs/risc0-bigint2/latest/src/risc0_bigint2/field/mod.rs.html)). OpenVM hints a non-residue at setup, uses `div_unsafe` for slopes, and loops forever on a bad hint ([OpenVM ECC audit](https://github.com/openvm-org/openvm/blob/main/audits/v1/v1-internal/extension-ecc.md)). Jolt exposes `DIVQ`/`DIVR` inlines that take the quotient as advice and a `SECP256K1_GLVR_ADV` inline that takes the GLV split `(k1, k2)` as "pure non-deterministic advice" checked against `k = k1 + k2·λ` ([a16z/jolt secp256k1 inline](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/a16z/jolt/main/jolt-inlines/secp256k1/src/lib.rs)). Polygon's zkASM obtains `r⁻¹`, `y` and every intermediate point of `k1·G + k2·R` as free inputs, alias-checks each, and verifies each point with a 32-row arithmetic-SM operation ([ecrecover.zkasm](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/0xPolygonHermez/zkevm-rom/main/main/ecrecover/ecrecover.zkasm); [mulPointEc.zkasm](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/0xPolygonHermez/zkevm-rom/main/main/ecrecover/mulPointEc.zkasm)). Your `Secp256k1HooksWithOracle` sits squarely in this family, with the one refinement several others lack: hinting the division rather than the inverse, which is what makes affine group operations cost one check plus two or three multiplications instead of the 7–11 of Jacobian formulas ([field_ops.rs](file:///Users/alexvlasov/MatterLabs/zksync-os/basic_system/src/system_functions/field_ops.rs); [hooks.rs](file:///Users/alexvlasov/MatterLabs/airbender-platform/crates/airbender-crypto/src/secp256k1/hooks.rs)).

What no system does is hint `u1·G + u2·R` itself and verify it more cheaply than recomputing it. The closest designs witness the public key and run signature verification instead of recovery (Scroll's `sig_circuit` passes `Q` as a private witness to `ecdsa_verify_no_pubkey_check` ([sig_circuit.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scroll-tech/zkevm-circuits/develop/zkevm-circuits/src/sig_circuit.rs)); early SP1 documentation motivated `unconstrained` with exactly this trade ([sp1-lib 1.2.0](https://docs.rs/sp1-lib/1.2.0))), but in naive form that costs the same two scalar multiplications. The only "whole multiplication as one precompile" proposal found is SP1 PR #2719's septic-curve Schnorr, which is not secp256k1 and whose AIR constraints "are not implemented" ([SP1 PR 2719](https://github.com/succinctlabs/sp1/pull/2719)). The per-step floor is therefore the hinted slope: given `P1`, `P2` and a claimed `P3`, the cheapest sound binding is `λ(x2−x1) = y2−y1`, `x3 = λ²−x1−x2`, `y3 = λ(x1−x3)−y1`, i.e. 2M+1S, and collinearity-plus-on-curve alternatives cost more. Hints cannot lower the operation count; they lower the cost per operation to ≈3–4 M-eq, and you are already there.

The reported costs below put your 66k cycles in context. They are in incomparable units, but the group-operation structure behind them is comparable: SP1's 783 syscalls per recovery match a double-and-add over both full scalars with "2L doublings and up to 2L additions" and no shared doublings ([sp1-lib utils.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/sp1-lib/latest/source/src/utils.rs)), which is ≈4× your ≈190 operations; RISC Zero's ≈650k cycles are Jacobian `lincomb` over syscall-wrapped modmuls ([Anoma report](https://forum.anoma.net/t/execution-proof-performance-report/2654)). Your stack already captures GLV, interleaving, wNAF-5/wNAF-10 and the fused step; the remaining wins must come from changing the equation, not the arithmetic.

The soundness record of this model is worth carrying into any new hint. The failures found in audits are all of one shape: a hint checked for the wrong property, or a failed check that an honest run could also produce. OpenVM's internal audit found a malicious host could make a valid decompression panic (finding 2.3) and out-of-range `r`,`s` made the circuit unprovable instead of returning an error (2.4) ([OpenVM ECC audit](https://github.com/openvm-org/openvm/blob/main/audits/v1/v1-internal/extension-ecc.md)); OpenVM's pairing hint `c` was tested only for `c ≠ 0`, letting a prover pass any pairing check with `c = 1` (CVE-2026-46669, Critical) ([zkSecurity](https://blog.zksecurity.xyz/posts/openvm-bugs/)); the USENIX '25 study of Polygon's free inputs found seven new vulnerabilities and named the "dual execution path attack" ([Peng et al.](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity25/presentation/peng-xinghao)); Jolt moved the `z, r, s < n`, coordinate-range and on-curve checks inside `ecdsa_verify()` after relying on callers proved to be "a security footgun" ([Jolt PR 1402](https://github.com/a16z/jolt/pull/1402)). The witnessed-key techniques below add three new hints (`Q`, a lattice vector, batch coefficients); each needs a side condition that is not "non-zero" but a specific bound or membership, and each failed check must stay a panic, never an `Err`.

## Coordinate tricks, windows and tables are exhausted, with under 5% left

Per group operation, hinted affine is unbeatable once a division costs one multiplication. Eisenträger–Lauter–Montgomery's affine table gives addition 1D+1M+1S, doubling 1D+1M+2S, and the combined `2P±Q` at 2D+1M+2S by skipping the intermediate `y` ([ELM, CT-RSA 2003](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0208038)); your `recover_affine.rs` implements exactly that fusion ("two divisions, two squarings and one multiplication") ([recover_affine.rs](file:///Users/alexvlasov/MatterLabs/airbender-platform/crates/airbender-crypto/src/secp256k1/recover_affine.rs)). The inversion-free alternatives exist only to avoid inversion and lose once it is free: libsecp256k1's Jacobian doubling is 3M+4S and mixed addition 8M+3S ([group_impl.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin-core/secp256k1/master/src/group_impl.h)), the co-Z ladder step is 11M+5S per bit ([ePrint 2010/309](https://eprint.iacr.org/2010/309)), the x-only Weierstrass ladder step is 8M+7S plus small-constant products ([EFD XZ](https://hyperelliptic.org/EFD/g1p/auto-shortw-xz.html)) and cannot share doublings with a second scalar, so a two-dimensional differential chain at ≈1.4 additions + 1.4 doublings per bit ([Azarderakhsh–Karabina](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/techreports/2012/cacr2012-24.pdf)) lands near 21 M-eq per bit against ≈4.7 for your wNAF-5 affine schedule. Montgomery's simultaneous-inversion trick is counterproductive here: it trades `n` one-multiplication checks for one check plus `3(n−1)` real multiplications ([batch affine vs mixed add](https://hackmd.io/@jsign/batch-affine-vs-mixed-add)). Montgomery and Edwards models require a point of order 2 and a group order divisible by 4 ([Montgomery curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_curve)), which secp256k1's prime order rules out, and because isogenous curves share their point count no isogeny escapes this.

Windows are at their optimum too. libsecp256k1 fixes `WINDOW_A = 5` as "optimal for 128-bit and 256-bit exponents" ([ecmult_impl.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin-core/secp256k1/master/src/ecmult_impl.h)), and the arithmetic agrees: w = 4 costs 4 table ops + 51.6 additions, w = 5 costs 8 + 43.0, w = 6 costs 16 + 36.9. For the generator, your two 256-entry tables of `G` and `2^128·G` (32 KiB, `WINDOW_G = 10`) ([context.rs](file:///Users/alexvlasov/MatterLabs/airbender-platform/crates/airbender-crypto/src/secp256k1/context.rs)) give ≈23.5 additions; going to libsecp256k1's default `ECMULT_WINDOW_SIZE = 15` (1 MiB of tables, "two tables of this size … due to the endomorphism optimization" ([ecmult.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin-core/secp256k1/master/src/ecmult.h))) saves only ≈7 group operations, about 4%, for a 32× larger table whose memory traffic has its own proving cost. A signed-digit comb for `G` (libsecp256k1's 22 kB default: 44 additions and 3 doublings ([ecmult_gen.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin-core/secp256k1/master/src/ecmult_gen.h))) is worse than wNAF-10's 24 additions because your 128 doublings are already shared with the variable-base part; combs pay off only for stand-alone or constant-time fixed-base multiplication. The 128 doublings are set by the longest scalar after decomposition, and the operation count can only fall by shortening scalars.

The field layer deserves one remark for each of your two paths. With delegation, a multiplication costs **27.1 cycles and 6.25 `bigint_with_control` calls**, a squaring 30.3 cycles and 7.3 calls ([ecrecover_bench README](file:///Users/alexvlasov/MatterLabs/zksync-os/binaries/ecrecover_bench/README.md)); at ≈550–600 M-eq per recovery that is ≈16k of your 66k cycles, so roughly 50k cycles are hint fetches, normalisation, additions, wNAF recoding, scalar arithmetic, table set-up and control flow, around 200 cycles per group operation on top of ≈95 cycles of multiplication. That overhead is per-operation and so shrinks with the lattice and batch techniques, but it also means a dedicated 256-bit modular-multiply delegation (the shape of RISC Zero's ≈10-cycle modmul ([RISC Zero optimization guide](https://www.mintlify.com/risc0/risc0/advanced/optimization)) or SP1's `syscall_uint256_mulmod`) would cut the 7,451 calls by several times independently of any algorithmic change; that is an Airbender circuit question rather than a guest one. For the software fallback, libsecp256k1's 10×26 multiplication is ≈122 multiply-accumulates (100 limb products plus 22 constant products for the `2^260 ≡ 2^36 + 0x3D10` fold) ([field_10x26_impl.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bitcoin-core/secp256k1/master/src/field_10x26_impl.h)) and measures 1,091 cycles on the Airbender simulator; an 8×32 schoolbook with the special-prime fold needs ≈72 products but explicit carry chains, and RV32IM lacks the single-cycle `UMAAL` that lets Cortex-M4 code reach 284 cycles per multiplication in the neighbouring field 2^255−19 ([Fujii–Aranha](https://sol.sbc.org.br/index.php/sbseg_estendido/article/download/4142/4071)). No published secp256k1 figure exists for plain RV32IM; expect at most a factor of two from a tuned 8×32 kernel, and only invest if the 826k-cycle path is a production path.

## Witnessing the key and reducing a rank-4 lattice cuts the chain to ~64 doublings

The one formulation that beats the baseline for a single recovery starts from Antipa, Brown, Gallant, Lambert, Struik and Vanstone (SAC 2005). Their observation is that the verification equation "may be replaced by any (nonzero) scalar multiple hereof", `(v·e·s⁻¹)G + (v·r·s⁻¹)Q − vR = O`, and that choosing `v` so that both `v` and `u := v·r·s⁻¹ mod n` have half the bit-length of `n` (the extended Euclidean algorithm on `(n, r/s)` stopped at the first remainder below `√n`) halves the point doublings, a 40% speed-up on P-384 and 36% measured on an ARM7TDMI ([Antipa et al., CACR 2005-28](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/techreports/2005/cacr2005-28.pdf); [Struik slides](https://www.fields.utoronto.ca/programs/scientific/07-08/cryptography/rene_struik.pdf)). The method needs the full point `R`, which for standard ECDSA means "a single bit of side information" when `n > q` and two bits otherwise ([Antipa et al. §4.2](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/techreports/2005/cacr2005-28.pdf)); Ethereum's recovery id is exactly those bits, so an ecrecover verifier is always in Antipa's favourable case. Yet on secp256k1 the 2-D form alone gains nothing: it yields four ≈128-bit scalars and 128 doublings, which is what GLV already gives you, matching Struik's restriction of the 40% to "non-Koblitz curves" and the paper's remark that endomorphism curves see "only a marginal improvement". The same arithmetic shows up in the in-circuit measurements: "fake GLV" cut P-256 `[s]P` by 47.8% and full P-256 ECDSA by 34.6%, but Bandersnatch, whose baseline already had GLV, moved only 1.4% ([ethresear.ch fake GLV](https://ethresear.ch/t/fake-glv-you-dont-need-an-efficient-endomorphism-to-implement-glv-like-scalar-multiplication-in-snark-circuits/20394)).

The gain comes from stacking the two decompositions. Eagen's suggestion in that thread, implemented by El Housni, applies GLV first and then runs the half-GCD in the CM order (Eisenstein integers for `j = 0`, which secp256k1 is), producing four scalars "all about r^{1/4}" and the check `[u0]P + [u1]φ(P) − [v0]Q − [v1]φ(Q) = O` ([ethresear.ch, comments](https://ethresear.ch/t/fake-glv-you-dont-need-an-efficient-endomorphism-to-implement-glv-like-scalar-multiplication-in-snark-circuits/20394)). El Housni's slides bound each scalar at `n/4 + 9` bits and report **secp256k1 `[k]P` at 385,461 → 282,223 PLONKish constraints (−26.8%) over ordinary GLV** ([ZK Hub slides](https://yelhousni.github.io/zkhub.pdf)); the LATINCRYPT 2025 paper generalises to multi-scalar multiplication "as used in signature verification" with 22–53% proving-time gains over the prior state of the art ([ePrint 2025/933](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/933)). For recovery the equation is: find `(a, b, c, d)` with `(a + bλ)·r ≡ (c + dλ)·s (mod n)`, then check `a·R + b·φ(R) − c·Q − d·φ(Q) − ((a + bλ)·z·s⁻¹ mod n)·G = O`. The lattice `{(a,b,c,d) : (a+bλ)r ≡ (c+dλ)s}` has index `n` in `Z⁴`, so Minkowski guarantees a nonzero vector with every coordinate at most `n^{1/4} = 2^64`, and exact shortest-vector search in dimension 4 is trivial on the host; the half-GCD heuristic gives up to the +9 bits of the slides. In your cost model the schedule becomes ≈64–73 shared doublings, four variable-base wNAF-5 streams (`R`, `φ(R)`, `Q`, `φ(Q)`) at ≈43–49 additions plus two 8-entry odd-multiple tables (16 operations and 16 multiplications by β), and the full-length scalar on `G` split into four 64-bit chunks over tables for `G`, `2^64·G`, `2^128·G`, `2^192·G` (64 KiB at `WINDOW_G = 10`) at ≈24 additions: **≈147–162 group operations against ≈190–203 today, roughly 395 M-eq against 550**, a 20–28% reduction consistent with the measured −26.8%. These counts are this report's derivation from the formulas above, not a published secp256k1 measurement; the 4-D ECDSA numbers in ePrint 2025/933 could not be retrieved.

Soundness is clean because secp256k1 has prime order and cofactor 1. With `r, s ∈ [1, n−1]` and `R` fixed by `(r, v)`, the equation `sR = zG + rQ` determines `Q` uniquely (Antipa's Theorem 2 states the equivalence of the original and `R`-transmitting schemes), so a witnessed `Q` that passes the scaled equation is the recovered key; the scaling is invertible because `v = a + bλ ≠ 0 mod n` follows from the bounds: `a + bλ ≡ 0` with `λ² + λ + 1 ≡ 0` forces the norm `a² − ab + b² ≡ 0 (mod n)`, impossible for nonzero `|a|,|b| < 2^65` since the norm is below `n`. El Housni's cofactor-torsion attacks on hinted scalar multiplications, which accept `Q' = [k]P + T` for small torsion `T` on BLS12-381, BN254 and BW6-761 ([ePrint 2026/1776](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1776)), do not reach a cofactor-1 curve provided `R` and `Q` are checked on the curve, which is 2S+1M each. The guest-side checklist is therefore: `r, s` in range; `y(R)` hinted and checked by `y² = r³ + 7` plus parity against `v`, with the existing `candidate² = −x` non-residue proof for the no-root case; `Q` hinted (64 bytes), on-curve, not the identity; `(a, b, c, d)` hinted as signed 65-bit integers, range-checked, with the congruence verified by a few mod-`n` multiplications (hinted quotients, or the delegation); and the `Q = O` outcome (`sR = zG`) proven by a direct fixed-base check rather than asserted, since a prover must never be able to claim failure on a valid input. The complete formulas you already have remain mandatory: with four caller-influenced bases, an accumulator equal to `±` a table entry is reachable.

The neighbours of this technique do not improve on it. The 3-dimensional decomposition of Suchanek, Sys and Chmielewski gives scalars of a third the size (≈86 bits) without witnessing `Q`, about 20% in native Schnorr verification, "provided that one additional bit of information is appended" ([ePrint 2025/1151](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1151)); with `Q` already witnessed, 4-D dominates it. Antipa's "Case 2" (six scalars of `n^{1/3}` using a second precomputed multiple of `Q`) has no hint-model analogue because verifying `2^{t/2}·Q` costs a scalar multiplication. Eagen's ECIP represents points summing to zero by a function-field element checked at a random divisor via Weil reciprocity, with claimed ≈100× fewer multiplications in SNARK settings ([ePrint 2022/596](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/596)), and in R1CS "an MSM of arbitrary length can be verified in 7 multiplicative constraints" once committed ([zkSecurity divisor notes](https://blog.zksecurity.xyz/posts/divisor-notes/)), but that counts only multiplicative constraints where linear work is free and needs a polynomial commitment to source the challenge; in the one native implementation with published numbers, Garaga's Cairo MSM costs ≈780 MulMod per secp256k1 point and 1,592 for an ECDSA verification ([Garaga README](https://github.com/keep-starknet-strange/garaga)), above both your current ≈550 M-eq and the 4-D ≈395. Personae's `s·T + U = Q` rearrangement only moves `T = r⁻¹R` to a party whose cost is not counted ([Personae post](https://digital-garden-bpz.pages.dev/blog/efficient-ecdsa)); in a guest it is itself a variable-base multiplication. Cycles of curves make base-field arithmetic native inside a Spartan-style proof over secq256k1 (3,039 constraints) ([spartan-ecdsa](https://github.com/personaelabs/spartan-ecdsa)) and Aztec's Goblin/ECCVM defers BN254 operations to a Grumpkin VM ([ECCVM README](https://barretenberg.aztec.network/api/md_src_2barretenberg_2eccvm_2_r_e_a_d_m_e)), but a RISC-V guest with a bigint delegation already has "native" 256-bit arithmetic and would gain nothing; folding keeps per-signature arithmetic constant at ≈4,000 R1CS in BEATS, removing recursion overhead rather than curve work ([BEATS, FCS 2026](https://journal.hep.com.cn/fcs/EN/10.1007/s11704-025-41269-5)); and secp256k1 is immune to MOV/Frey–Rück transfer ([SafeCurves](https://safecurves.cr.yp.to/transfer.html)), so no pairing check exists.

## One Pippenger MSM per block cuts per-signature work 2–5× but makes the address a hint until block end

Batching is the only lever with a multiplicative payoff, and ecrecover is the favourable case the batching literature spent a decade wishing for. Random-linear-combination batch verification (Naccache et al. 1994, Bellare–Garay–Rabin 1998) needs the full nonce point `R_i`, which standard ECDSA withholds; Bernstein et al. note the proposal "is not directly applicable to … ECDSA … but if R is transmitted … this problem disappears" ([Ed25519 paper](https://ed25519.cr.yp.to/ed25519-20110926.pdf)), and the standard-ECDSA papers that fought the `±y` ambiguity symbolically topped out at batches of 7–9 with a 6.2× best case for a single signer ([Karati et al., JCEN 2014](https://cse.iitkgp.ac.in/~abhij/publications/ECDSA-JCEN.pdf)). With the recovery id fixing `R_i` and `Q_i` witnessed, the batch check is the Ed25519-style equation with `2n + 1` scalars, half of them 128-bit: after scaling each equation by `c_i/s_i`, verify `Σ c_i·R_i − Σ (c_i·r_i/s_i)·Q_i − (Σ c_i·z_i/s_i)·G = O` as one MSM. Pippenger's bucket method costs ≈`(λ/c)(N + 2^c)` additions plus `λ` doublings ([Pippenger note](https://hackmd.io/@tazAymRSQCGXTUKkbh1BAg/Sk27liTW9)); BDLO measured Bos–Coster at 0.55·nb, ≈70 additions per signature at n = 64, against ≈358–384 for separate Straus/JSF verification, and state that Pippenger "is almost twice as fast in some cases" and within 1+o(1) of optimal ([BDLO 2012](https://cr.yp.to/badbatch/badbatch-20120919.pdf)). The worked model for the ecrecover scalar layout (signed buckets, shared windows, 256 doublings per batch) gives **≈96 operations per signature at n = 100 (c = 6), ≈56 at n = 1,000 (c = 8) and ≈41 at n = 10,000 (c = 11)**, i.e. 2.0×, 3.5× and 4.8× below your ≈190–203; applying GLV inside the batch adds only a few per cent (≈82 vs 96 at n = 100) because bucket insertions, not doublings, dominate, and the Antipa/4-D trick is subsumed since every scalar is already arbitrary. Below n ≈ 16–32 the batch loses to per-signature GLV (BDLO's Straus batch measured 1.15·nb at n = 16), so the design needs a per-proof threshold. No production zkEVM or L1 zkVM was found to batch ecrecover; the only in-circuit precedent, circom `batch-ecdsa`, reached 478k constraints per signature at batch 32 from 1.5M using Straus-style shared doublings rather than buckets ([batch-ecdsa](https://github.com/puma314/batch-ecdsa)), and the Anoma report lists aggregation as the one lever left on RISC Zero ([Anoma report](https://forum.anoma.net/t/execution-proof-performance-report/2654)).

The soundness requirements are exact and inexpensive. BGR98 Theorem 3.3 gives error `2^-l` for independent uniform `l`-bit coefficients in a prime-order group and warns that in non-prime-order groups the test "do[es] not work; it is easy to find counter-examples" ([BGR98](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~mihir/papers/batch.pdf)); secp256k1's cofactor 1 means on-curve checks of every `R_i` and `Q_i` suffice for the prime-order hypothesis. BDLO's two attacks on the un-randomised scheme, cancelling `(r, s)` against `(r, −s)` and forging under any target key given one secret key, fix what the transcript must contain: the coefficients must be derived after, and from, every `r_i, s_i, z_i, v_i`, the hinted `y(R_i)` and the hinted `Q_i`, and BDLO "emphasize the importance of including these randomizers" with 128 bits ([BDLO 2012](https://cr.yp.to/badbatch/badbatch-20120919.pdf)). Powers `t^i` of a single challenge cost `n·2^-l` by Schwartz–Zippel, so with a 128-bit `t` the loss is `log₂ n` bits; independent 128-bit coefficients from a hash chain avoid even that. The prover controls the witness and can re-sample freely, so grinding multiplies `2^-l` by its hash budget: 64-bit coefficients would buy ≈64 bits minus grinding, which is why production batch verifiers (ed25519-zebra, frost-core, decaf377-rdsa) draw fresh 128-bit coefficients per signature ([ed25519-zebra batch.rs](https://docs.rs/ed25519-zebra/1.0.1/src/ed25519_zebra/batch.rs.html)). The MSM cost difference between 128- and 256-bit coefficients on the `R_i` terms is only ≈15–20%, so there is no reason to go below 128.

The architectural cost is where the decision lies. A batch check can run only after the last recovery of the proof is known, but the bootloader needs each sender address and each `ecrecover` precompile result immediately, because the address decides the execution path. The batch design therefore is the witnessed-key design taken to its conclusion: at call time the guest takes `Q_i` from the oracle, checks it on-curve and non-identity, derives the address and continues; it records `(z_i, r_i, s_i, v_i, y_i, Q_i)` (≈200 bytes, so ≈2 MB for 10,000 signatures); at block end it hashes the records into coefficients, performs the `n` hinted scalar inversions `s_i⁻¹` (one multiplication check each), runs Pippenger over hinted-affine complete additions, and panics if the sum is not the identity, so a wrong witness yields no proof rather than a provable failure, the same rule SP1 encodes as exit code 3 and OpenVM as an infinite loop. The three failure outcomes keep their own per-signature proofs: out-of-range `r`,`s` by comparison, "no square root" by the existing `candidate² = −x` witness, and `Q_i = O` by simply dropping the `Q_i` term from that signature's equation, since `c_i(s_i R_i − z_i G) = O` is still a linear relation the batch covers. Caller-crafted collisions (`R_i = ±Q_j`, repeated keys) are reachable and land in the same bucket, so the bucket accumulation must use the complete formulas rather than the "unequal" shortcut. The payoff scales with the number of signatures per proof; under a per-tx-range or small-block proving layout the threshold matters more than the asymptote.

Rank 1 before rank 2 is not only about risk: the lattice variant builds the entire witnessed-key machinery (the `Q` hint, on-curve and identity checks, the fixed-base proof of the `Q = O` outcome, the discipline that a failed check is a panic) that the batch needs, so it is the natural first milestone even if the batch is the destination. Rank 1 also leaves the protocol boundary untouched: the recovered address is still established before the call returns, so nothing downstream changes. Rank 2 changes what a recovered address means mid-block and should be scoped as a protocol change with its own validation of the transcript derivation, following the "Treat behavior changes as protocol changes" rule rather than as a crypto-crate optimisation.

## Conclusion

The survey changes the framing of the problem from "verify a scalar multiplication more cheaply" to "choose which linear relation over the group the guest checks". Hints already reduced every group operation to its algebraic floor of a slope check and two identities; what remains free to choose is the relation itself, and the shortest-vector view makes the limit explicit. For one recovery the verifier's cost is set by the shortest vector in the lattice of relations it is willing to check, `n^{1/2}` per extra variable base and `n^{1/4}` once the endomorphism is used, and no cheaper relation is known for a verifier restricted to group operations plus `φ`; nothing in the literature proves this is optimal, and the only candidate that evades generic-group counting, ECIP, is not cheaper in native multiplications today. For many recoveries the relation is one random combination and the cost falls like `1/log n`, which is the only path to a step change.

Two implications follow for the stack. First, because field multiplication is now ≈16k of 66k cycles, algorithmic savings and overhead savings compound rather than compete: every group operation removed takes ≈300 cycles with it, not ≈95, so a 25% reduction in operations is worth roughly 25% of the recovery, and a profile of the remaining per-operation overhead is cheap insurance before either rewrite. Second, witnessing the key moves ecrecover from a computation into a claim checked later, and the audit record shows that the failures of this model are never in the arithmetic but at the boundary between hint and check: the side condition on the lattice vector, the completeness of the transcript, and the rule that no bad hint may ever surface as an honest outcome.
