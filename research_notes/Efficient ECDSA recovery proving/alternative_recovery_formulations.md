# Alternative mathematical formulations of ECDSA recovery/verification for a hint-checking verifier (secp256k1 / ecrecover)

Scope note. "Verifier" below means the party that must be convinced (in our case the proven RISC-V guest, or a circuit); "hints" are prover-supplied values that cost roughly one field multiplication each to check. Cost unit: M = multiplication in F_p (secp256k1 base field), S = squaring (treated as ~1 M), D/A = point doubling / addition. Notation: n = group order, G = generator, (r, s) signature, z = message hash, R = ephemeral point with x(R) = r (or r + n), Q = public key; recovery computes Q = r^{-1}(s R - z G); verification checks s R = z G + r Q.

## Key question 1 — Is any known formulation cheaper than ~128 doublings + additions of the variable base (GLV-Straus recovery baseline)?

### Takeaway
Yes, one is known and documented in two independent lines of work: witness Q and verify the linear relation s R − z G − r Q = O after multiplying it by a small scalar v chosen so that the coefficients on the *variable* points R and Q are short. Antipa–Brown–Gallant–Lambert–Struik–Vanstone (SAC 2005) gives half-length (√n) scalars via the extended Euclidean algorithm; combining it with the secp256k1 endomorphism (El Housni–Piellard "fake GLV", 2024, with Eagen's 4D idea; Eagen–El Housni–Masson–Piellard, LATINCRYPT 2025) gives four scalars of ≈ n^{1/4} ≈ 64 (+9) bits on the bases {R, φ(R), Q, φ(Q)}, i.e. ≈ 64–73 doublings instead of 128, with a measured ≈27% constraint reduction over ordinary GLV for a single secp256k1 scalar multiplication. No formulation with o(√(security parameter)) group operations on the variable base is known to me; the only known "sublinear-looking" alternative (Eagen's ECIP / divisor technique) is, in the one native-cost implementation with published numbers (Garaga), *not* cheaper than the GLV baseline for 1–3 points (~780 modular multiplications per point).

### Cited Findings
- Core transformation (Antipa et al. 2005, §4): the verification equation s^{-1}(eG + rQ) = R "may be replaced by any (nonzero) scalar multiple hereof": (v e s^{-1}) G + (v r s^{-1}) Q − v R = O (their eq. (3)); "if one chooses v such that both v and u := v r s^{-1} (mod n) are integers that are significantly smaller than n, such as having only half the bit-length of n, one can often considerably speed-up the computation". The cost "usually depends on the maximum bit-size over all scalar multiples corresponding to unknown points", while the fixed point G can use precomputation — [Antipa et al., CACR 2005-28 / SAC 2005](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/techreports/2005/cacr2005-28.pdf); [Springer LNCS 3897 pp. 307–318](https://rd.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/11693383_21.pdf); [dblp](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/sacrypt/AntipaBGLSV05).
- Existence of short (u, v) is Dirichlet/lattice: Corollary 5 "v(α1,…,αℓ) ≡ (u1,…,uℓ) (mod n) has an integer solution with |u1|,…,|uℓ| < n/B and v ∈ [1, B^ℓ]"; Example 6 (ℓ = 1): any x ∈ Z_n is x ≡ u/v with |u| < √n, 1 ≤ v ≤ √n, found by running the extended Euclidean algorithm on (n, x) and stopping "at that iteration … when r_i first becomes less than √n" (then |t_i| is "very likely … also close to √n"); for ℓ = 2, 3 one uses low-dimensional lattice reduction (Nguyen–Stehlé) — [Antipa et al. 2005, §3](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/techreports/2005/cacr2005-28.pdf).
- Resulting operation counts (Antipa et al., §4.1, JSF-based multi-scalar multiplication, m = bit length): original equation λG + µQ = R costs ≈ (m/2 + 2)A + mD; transformed equation λ0 G0 + λ1 G1 + uQ − vR = O with all four scalars ~√n costs ≈ (m/2 + 4)A + (m/2)D ("Case 1", one multiple of Q available). With a second precomputed multiple of Q ("Case 2"): six scalars ~ n^{1/3} and cost ≈ (m/2 + 6)A + (m/3)D. For P-384 in Jacobian coordinates (A ≈ 10.4 M, D ≈ 7.2 M): Case 1 goes from ≈ 194A + 384D ≈ 459A to 196A + 192D ≈ 328A, "a speed-up of 40%"; Case 2 from 328A to 198A + 128D ≈ 286A (15%). ARM7TDMI measurement on P-384: 209 ms → 154 ms (36%) — [Antipa et al. 2005](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/techreports/2005/cacr2005-28.pdf).
- Side information needed to apply this to standard ECDSA (not ECDSA*): if n > q the x-coordinate of R equals r and "a single bit of side information is sufficient to efficiently determine the y-coordinate of R"; if n < q "the x-coordinate of R is equal to r or r + n … Thus, in this case, two bits of side information are sufficient" — [Antipa et al. 2005, §4.2](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/techreports/2005/cacr2005-28.pdf). Ethereum/Bitcoin recovery ids already carry exactly these two bits (parity of y, and whether x = r or r + n) — [eosjs-ecc implementation notes citing SEC 1 §4.1.6](https://app.unpkg.com/eosjs-ecc@4.0.7/files/lib/ecdsa.js); [Bitcoin Core ecwrapper annotated "SEC1 4.1.6"](https://git.boxtec.ch/boxtec/bitcoin/src/commit/e4e5334ef8d923993ef9cf704ea8f3d0b5801350/src/ecwrapper.cpp).
- Same trick rediscovered for SNARK circuits as "fake GLV" (El Housni, Nov 2024): "We can 'hint' the result Q and check in-circuit that" s·P − Q = O; with u, v ≤ √r and v·s ≡ u (mod r) the check becomes u·P − v·Q = O, "a two-term multi-scalar multiplication with small scalars"; the half-GCD is "free through the hint"; the post credits SAC05 (ABGL+) for the ECDSA case — [ethresear.ch post](https://ethresear.ch/t/fake-glv-you-dont-need-an-efficient-endomorphism-to-implement-glv-like-scalar-multiplication-in-snark-circuits/20394); [HackMD companion](https://hackmd.io/@yelhousni/fake-glv).
- Measured savings of fake GLV (gnark, emulated arithmetic in a BN254 PLONK/R1CS): P-256 [s]P 738,031 → 385,412 scs (−47.8%) and 186,466 → 100,914 R1CS (−45.9%); P-256 ECDSA verification 1,135,876 → 742,541 scs (−34.6%), 293,814 → 195,266 R1CS (−33.5%); STARK curve ECDSA 1,137,459 → 732,131 scs; Jubjub [s]P 5,863 → 4,549; Bandersnatch (baseline already GLV) 4,781 → 4,712 (−1.4%) — [ethresear.ch post](https://ethresear.ch/t/fake-glv-you-dont-need-an-efficient-endomorphism-to-implement-glv-like-scalar-multiplication-in-snark-circuits/20394).
- 4D variant (Eagen's comment, implemented by El Housni): apply GLV s = s0 + λ s1, then run a half-GCD in the CM order (Eisenstein integers for j = 0 curves such as secp256k1) on s0 + α s1 modulo r0 + α r1; the four scalars are "all about r^{1/4} (up to a constant factor)"; lattice L = {(u0,u1,v0,v1,q) : u0 + u1α − v0 s − v1 s α + r q = 0}, so LLL/BKZ can replace the Euclidean-domain requirement; the check is [u0]P + [u1]φ(P) − [v0]Q − [v1]φ(Q) = O — [ethresear.ch post, comments](https://ethresear.ch/t/fake-glv-you-dont-need-an-efficient-endomorphism-to-implement-glv-like-scalar-multiplication-in-snark-circuits/20394). Slides give the bound "For 128-bit security n/4+9 bits" per scalar and benchmarks (BN254-PLONK emulated, scs): secp256k1 scalar mul 385,461 (ordinary GLV) → 282,223 (4D fake GLV), i.e. −26.8%; BN254 381,467 → 279,262; BLS12-381 539,973 → 390,294; BW6-761 1,367,067 → 1,010,785 — [El Housni, ZK Hub Bangkok slides, 11 Nov 2024](https://yelhousni.github.io/zkhub.pdf).
- Formal version: "Fast elliptic curve scalar multiplications in SN(T)ARK circuits", Eagen, El Housni, Masson, Piellard, LATINCRYPT 2025 (ePrint 2025/933, received 2025-05-22, revised 2025-10-17): techniques "use integer lattice reduction or a (half) extended Euclidean algorithm in a ring of integers", with "optimizations for curves with small complex multiplication (CM) discriminants", extended "to multi-scalar multiplication, as used in signature verification"; reported proving-time speed-up "22% to 53% over the previous state of the art" — [ePrint 2025/933](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/933).
- Witnessed-Q verification is equivalent to recovery: Antipa et al. Theorem 2: "(r, s) is a valid signature … in the original signature scheme" iff "(R, s) is a valid signature … in the modified scheme for precisely one point R in the set ϕ(r) := {X ∈ G | f(X) = r}" — [Antipa et al. 2005, §2](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/techreports/2005/cacr2005-28.pdf). Recovery itself: SEC 1 §4.1.6 recovers Q = r^{-1}(sR − eG) for the candidate R — [Bitcoin covenants paper citing SEC 1 §4.1.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.16714).
- ECIP native cost data point (Garaga, Cairo, hint-based ECIP MSM): `test_msm_SECP256K1_1P` = 9,278 steps, 783 MulMod, 900 AddMod; `_2P` = 18,028 steps, 1,564 MulMod; `_3P` = 27,115 steps, 2,359 MulMod; `test_ecdsa_SECP256K1` = 18,822 steps, 1,592 MulMod, 1,810 AddMod; `test_schnorr_SECP256K1` = 1,580 MulMod — [Garaga README benchmarks](https://github.com/keep-starknet-strange/garaga) (raw: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/keep-starknet-strange/garaga/main/README.md). Garaga lists secp256k1 among its six MSM curves and cites ePrint 2022/596 and 2025/933 as references — [Garaga docs](https://garaga.gitbook.io/garaga).

### Inferences
- Cost model used throughout (my derivation from standard affine formulas): addition with hinted slope λ: check λ(x2 − x1) = y2 − y1 (1 M), x3 = λ² − x1 − x2 (1 S), y3 = λ(x1 − x3) − y1 (1 M) → 2 M + 1 S; to make the slope check sound when x1 = x2 one must additionally prove x1 ≠ x2 (hinted inverse, +1 M) or use the doubling branch. Doubling with hinted λ: 2y1·λ = 3x1² (1 M + 1 S), x3 = λ² − 2x1 (1 S), y3 (1 M) → 2 M + 2 S. So D ≈ A ≈ 3–4 M in the hint model, versus Antipa's Jacobian D ≈ 7.2 M, A ≈ 10.4 M; the D/A ratio is ~1, so doublings dominate less than in their analysis, but reducing scalar length still removes both doublings and additions proportionally.
- Baselines for ecrecover on secp256k1 with free fixed-base tables for G (my estimates): (a) plain Straus, 256-bit: ~256 D + ~128 A on R ≈ 1,400 M; (b) GLV on R (u2 = k1 + k2 λ, |k_i| ≈ 2^128), Straus over {R, φ(R)}: ~128 D + ~96 A + ~32 fixed-base A ≈ 900 M — this is the "~128 doublings + additions" baseline of the question.
- Antipa 2D with witnessed Q (no endomorphism): find (v, w) with v·r ≡ w·s (mod n), |v|,|w| ≲ √n, check v R − w Q − (v z s^{-1} mod n) G = O: 128 D + ~96 A on {R, Q} — the same doubling count as (b). On secp256k1 the Euclid trick alone therefore does *not* beat GLV recovery; it only helps on curves without an endomorphism (consistent with the fake-GLV table: −34% ECDSA on P-256 but −1.4% on Bandersnatch, whose baseline is already GLV).
- Stacking both: the 4D lattice L = {(a,b,c,d) ∈ Z^4 : (a + bλ) r ≡ (c + dλ) s (mod n)} has index n in Z^4, so Minkowski gives a nonzero vector with ‖·‖_∞ ≤ n^{1/4} ≈ 2^64 (El Housni's bound: n/4 + 9 bits via the Eisenstein half-GCD). Verifier check: a R + b φ(R) − c Q − d φ(Q) − (v z s^{-1} mod n) G = O with v = a + bλ. Cost estimate: ~64–73 D + ≤ 73 A (Straus over 4 bases with a 16-entry table costing ~11 A to build) + ~32 fixed-base A + 2 M for the two φ's ≈ 600–650 M, i.e. ~30% below baseline (b), matching the measured −26.8% for a single 4D scalar multiplication. The hint checks are cheap: the mod-n relation needs ~4 mod-n multiplications with hinted quotients; range checks on a, b, c, d; and v ≠ 0 mod n follows from (a, b) ≠ (0, 0): if a + bλ ≡ 0 then the norm a² − ab + b² ≡ 0 (mod n), and |a² − ab + b²| < 3·2^130 < n forces a = b = 0.
- Soundness/uniqueness: with the recovery id fixing R (x ∈ {r, r+n}, parity of y) and r, s ∈ [1, n−1], the equation s R = z G + r Q determines Q uniquely, so a witnessed Q that passes the (scaled) equation equals the recovered key; the verifier must also check Q is on the curve (y² = x³ + 7: ~3 M) because the point-addition identities are only meaningful for curve points. ecrecover failure cases (x not a valid x-coordinate; Q = O, i.e. s R = z G) must have their own hinted proofs (quadratic non-residuosity witness; a direct small-scalar check of s R − z G = O), otherwise a prover could falsely claim failure — this is a protocol-design detail, not a cost issue.
- Why nothing beats n^{1/4} with this family: the only free extra "multiple" of a variable point is φ (φ² = −1 − φ gives nothing new), so the lattice has rank 4 over Z and the shortest vector is ≈ n^{1/4}. Antipa's Case 2 (six third-size scalars) needs an *independent* precomputed multiple such as 2^{t/2}Q; in the hint model such a multiple would have to be verified, which costs a scalar multiplication, so it is not free. Including G's scalar in the lattice (6 unknowns, 3 congruences → n^{3/8} ≈ 96 bits) is worse than keeping G on free fixed-base tables.
- ECIP does not currently beat this in native cost: Garaga's ~780 MulMod per secp256k1 point and 1,592 MulMod for a full ECDSA verification are above my ~600–900 M estimates for the lattice/GLV approaches with hinted slopes; ECIP's advantages are in R1CS-style systems where linear work is free, in amortization over many points, and in witness size — not in per-signature native multiplication counts.

### Gaps
- I could not obtain the LATINCRYPT 2025 PDF (ePrint 2025/933 is behind a Cloudflare challenge for both the fetcher and the browser pane), so the exact 2-MSM/ECDSA construction and its secp256k1 ECDSA constraint counts in the 4D setting are not confirmed; only the single-scalar 4D numbers from the slides are.

- I found no published formulation with fewer than ≈ n^{1/4} bits on the variable bases, and no impossibility result either (see Q4).

## Key question 2 — What exactly does Antipa et al. (SAC 2005) achieve, and can a zk verifier exploit it with witnessed values? Later works (Struik, Cheon–Yi, fake GLV).

### Takeaway
Antipa et al. achieve ≈ 40% (P-384, Jacobian, no precomputation beyond one multiple of G) by halving the scalars on the two variable points via Euclid, requiring the full point R (ECDSA*) or 1–2 side bits; a zk/hint verifier can exploit it completely because R's y-coordinate, the Euclid output (u, v) and Q itself can all be hinted and checked with a handful of modular multiplications; the recovery id of Ethereum signatures already provides the required side information. Follow-ups: Struik's IETF ECDSA* drafts (2010, 2021), Cheon–Yi batch verification (PKC 2007), Karati et al. batch verification of standard ECDSA, and the 2024–2025 "fake GLV"/LATINCRYPT line.

### Cited Findings
- ECDSA* definition: signature is (R, s) instead of (r, s), verification checks R = s^{-1}(eG + rQ); "the original and the modified schemes are equally secure" (Theorem 2) — [Antipa et al. 2005, §2](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/techreports/2005/cacr2005-28.pdf). Struik's 2021 draft: ECDSA* outputs "the ordered pair (R,s), rather than the pair (r,s)", normalizes "If the ephemeral signing key R has odd parity of the y-coordinate, change (r,s) to (r,-s)", verification checks "-s*R + (e*G+r*Q)=O", and "efficient tricks allow reducing the bit-size of the scalars involved … (thereby effectively halving the required point doubling operations)"; claimed speed-up "~1.3x to more than 2x" (single vs batch) — [draft-struik-lamps-verification-friendly-ecdsa-01](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-struik-lamps-verification-friendly-ecdsa-01); 2010 precursor [draft-struik-ecc-efficiencies-00](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-struik-ecc-efficiencies/); Struik 2008 talk reports "40% speed-up compared to ordinary approach" — [Fields Institute slides](https://av.fields.utoronto.ca/programs/scientific/07-08/cryptography/rene_struik.pdf).
- Accelerated ECDSA procedure (Antipa §4.2): compute candidate points ϕ(r), filter by side information, write x := r/s ≡ u/v (mod n) with u, v small, compute S := (v·e s^{-1}) G + uQ − vR and accept iff S = O; without side information on a cofactor-1 curve there are t = 2 admissible points and the expected workload is (t+1)/2 = 1.5 ECDSA* verifications, "which is still less than a traditional verification"; the incremental cost per extra candidate is v(R − R′) — [Antipa et al. 2005](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/techreports/2005/cacr2005-28.pdf).
- Koblitz/endomorphism remark in the paper: "For Koblitz curves, we can only expect a marginal improvement, since for those curves the Frobenius map … comes almost for free" — the paper does not itself combine the trick with GLV on prime curves; that combination is the 2024–2025 4D work (Q1) — [Antipa et al. 2005, §4.1](https://cacr.uwaterloo.ca/techreports/2005/cacr2005-28.pdf); [ethresear.ch fake GLV](https://ethresear.ch/t/fake-glv-you-dont-need-an-efficient-endomorphism-to-implement-glv-like-scalar-multiplication-in-snark-circuits/20394).
- Cheon–Yi, "Fast Batch Verification of Multiple Signatures", PKC 2007 (LNCS 4450, pp. 442–457): batch verification for DSA*/ECDSA* using w-NAF exponents; "asymptotically seven and four times as fast as individual verification in case of a single signer and multiple signers"; for Koblitz curves "only six elliptic curve additions per each signature" (single signer) — [IACR archive PDF](https://www.iacr.org/archive/pkc2007/44500442/44500442.pdf); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-71677-8_29). Karati–Das et al. (Africacrypt 2012) present "the first reported study on the batch verification of standard ECDSA signatures" (recovering the missing y-coordinates algebraically) — [Africacrypt 2012 PDF](https://oldweb.isical.ac.in/~skarati/pub_pdf/ECDSApaper_Africacrypt12.pdf); [JCEN version](https://cse.iitkgp.ac.in/%7Eabhij/publications/ECDSA-JCEN.pdf).
- Fake GLV hint interface: prover supplies Q, u, v and a sign bit b ("By construction u is guaranteed to be a positive integer but v can be negative"; when b = 1 the circuit negates Q rather than reducing a negative v mod the SNARK field); the loop length is halved and Straus–Shamir is applied to the bits of u and v; ECDSA verification becomes "merely two fake GLV multiplications and an addition" — [ethresear.ch](https://ethresear.ch/t/fake-glv-you-dont-need-an-efficient-endomorphism-to-implement-glv-like-scalar-multiplication-in-snark-circuits/20394); [HackMD](https://hackmd.io/@yelhousni/fake-glv).
- Reference cost of the pre-fake-GLV state of the art in gnark: "a single secp256k1 signature verification is approximately 4M constraints in R1CS and 10M constraints in PLONKish" (older docs) — [gnark std/signature/ecdsa docs](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/consensys/gnark/std/signature/ecdsa); circom-ecdsa (0xPARC) ECDSAVerify: 1,508,136 R1CS constraints — [Personae efficient-ECDSA post](https://digital-garden-bpz.pages.dev/blog/efficient-ecdsa).

### Inferences
- In the hint model the "side information" is free: y(R) is a hinted field element checked by y² = r³ + 7 (≈3 M) plus a parity/overflow check against the recovery id; the Euclid/LLL output is hinted and checked by one congruence mod n. So the full Antipa speed-up (and the 4D extension) is available to a zk verifier at essentially zero overhead; nothing in the paper is verifier-side-expensive.
- Antipa's "Case 2" (a second multiple of Q from the certificate) has no hint-model analogue (see Q1), but the endomorphism plays its role on secp256k1.
- Struik's "more than 2x" refers to batch verification with a randomized linear combination; for a *single* ecrecover there is no batch, but if the guest verifies many signatures per block, a small-exponent batch check (Cheon–Yi style) over the already-hinted Q's could amortize the G-part and share doublings. Caveat (my note): batch tests need λ-bit random coefficients and hence λ doublings per batch plus one addition chain per signature, and a failure requires fallback identification; this is a separate design question.

### Gaps
- The paper gives no explicit statement combining the Euclid split with GLV for prime curves; the n^{1/4} result is only in the 2024–2025 sources.
- No numbers for the 2-MSM (ECDSA) 4D variant on secp256k1 were accessible (ePrint 2025/933 PDF blocked).

## Key question 3 — Witnessing intermediate points and checking only algebraic relations: per-step cost vs. computing affine additions with hinted slopes; probabilistic / divisor-based alternatives; Pippenger over a single signature.

### Takeaway
Witnessing every intermediate point of a double-and-add chain does not reduce cost below the hinted-slope arithmetic itself (each witnessed point still needs ≈ 3 M to be tied to its predecessors), so the per-step floor for chain-based verification is ≈ 2 M + 1 S per addition and ≈ 2 M + 2 S per doubling; windowing/Pippenger over a single scalar only trades additions for table building and cannot reduce the doubling count, which is set by the maximal scalar length. The only fundamentally different verification algebra is Eagen's ECIP (2022): a prover-supplied function-field element whose divisor encodes the MSM, checked at random challenge points via a logarithmic-derivative identity; in R1CS over a curve cycle it needs only a constant number of multiplicative constraints per scaling (Parker: 7 for a fixed generator), but its native cost in the one implementation with published numbers (Garaga) is ≈ 780 modular multiplications per secp256k1 point, i.e. not below the lattice/GLV chain.

### Cited Findings
- Eagen (ePrint 2022/596, May 2022): "To prove ECIPs more efficiently, it represents a collection of points that sum to zero using a polynomial element of the function field and evaluates this function at a random principal divisor. By Weil reciprocity, this is equal to the function interpolating the random divisor evaluated at the original points"; taking logarithmic derivatives lets the prover combine divisor witnesses "similar to the Bulletproofs++ permutation argument"; claimed "witness size is reduced by up to a factor of 10 and the number of multiplications by a factor of about 100", with the caveat that gains "depend on the instantiating proof system, number of curve points, and which information is zero knowledge" — [ePrint 2022/596](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/596).
- Hall-Andersen & Aranha (zkSecurity, April/May 2026, commissioned by MAGIC Grants) revisit the protocol: "the prover commits to the coefficients of f and the verifier checks that f has exactly that divisor"; "Done naively, this saves nothing over the simple group law emulation approach"; with Haböck's logarithmic-derivative trick "everything becomes a linear combination between public challenge values and the divisor polynomial f(X, Y)"; "an MSM of arbitrary length can be verified in 7 multiplicative constraints" (referring to Parker's R1CS gadget "for a 2k-bit scaling of a fixed generator in 7 multiplicative constraints", May 2024). Soundness uses an affine generalized Schwartz–Zippel bound; the note corrects prior work that applied the affine bound to projective varieties and provides a new extraction proof — [blog post](https://blog.zksecurity.xyz/posts/divisor-notes/); [PDF](https://blog.zksecurity.xyz/posts/divisor-notes/divisor-techniques.pdf).
- Caveat on the "7 constraints" figure from the gadget's author: a single divisor-based proof "does not actually prove the commitment has an i-bit value. The divisor may interpolate any of the i-powers of 2 up to i times", so range/decomposition constraints are extra — [kayabaNerve gist](https://gist.github.com/kayabaNerve/acdcd641fd930546ecd87743b06b37d8). Prover-side divisor construction was a bottleneck until the 2025 Monero contest ("95%+ improvement effectively renders divisors construction negligible") — [Monero FCMP++ contest results](https://web.getmonero.org/2025/08/27/fcmp++-contest-final.html).
- Native ECIP numbers (Garaga, Cairo with MulMod/AddMod builtins): secp256k1 MSM 1/2/3 points = 783 / 1,564 / 2,359 MulMod (≈ 780 per point); secp256r1 = 1,084 / 2,166 / 3,252; ECDSA secp256k1 = 1,592 MulMod + 1,810 AddMod + 18,822 steps — [Garaga README](https://github.com/keep-starknet-strange/garaga).
- Probabilistic (random-challenge) verification of delegated exponentiation: Di Crescenzo–Khodjaeva–Kahrobaei–Shpilrain (J. Math. Cryptol. 2021): "a product of m exponentiations in Z_p with σ-bit exponents can be delegated by a client that only uses less than 2λ + m + 4 modular multiplications in the online phase, if the probability of not detecting an incorrect result is bounded by 2^{−λ}", improving on 2mλ + 4m for repeated single delegation; results are for *fixed bases* with offline precomputation, and extend to EC groups (membership check "performs only 4 multiplications mod p" for the Weierstrass equation) — [JMC 2021 PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/151366/14/jmc_2019_0036_final.pdf). Other verifiable-outsourcing schemes for EC scalar multiplication: ExpSOS (Zhou–Afifi–Ren 2016) — [arXiv 1602.08472](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.08472); Tamura et al. 2009 server-aided scalar multiplication — [IEICE](https://www.ieice.org/ken/paper/20090925uaPn/eng/).
- Proofs of exponentiation: Wesolowski's proof "contains a single group element π, and the verifier needs to verify a single group identity y = π^s x^r, where s and r are ℓ-bit integers" — [ePrint 2024/145](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/145); statistically sound PoE in any group (Hoffmann–Hubáček–Kamath–Klein–Pietrzak, CRYPTO 2022) — [ISTA record](https://research-explorer.ista.ac.at/record/12176).
- Personae "efficient ECDSA" (2022/23): with R known, define T = r^{-1} R and U = −r^{-1} z G outside the circuit and check s·T + U = Q inside (one variable-base scalar multiplication plus one addition); with precomputed multiples (2^{8i}·j)·T, i ∈ [0,31], j ∈ [0,255], the circom circuit drops from 1,508,136 to 163,239 constraints — [Personae post](https://digital-garden-bpz.pages.dev/blog/efficient-ecdsa). Spartan-ecdsa (2023) over the secq256k1 cycle: "3,039 constraints for efficient ECDSA signature verification", 8,076 total for group membership; the yAcademy audit reports "an overall reduction of R1CS constraints from 1.5M to ~5k" and a high-severity missing-constraint issue later fixed — [spartan-ecdsa README mirror](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fpersonaelabs%2Fspartan-ecdsa); [yAcademy report](https://dev-reports-electisec.netlify.app/reports/06-2023-Spartan-ECDSA).

### Inferences
- "Witnessed intermediate points + algebraic checks" is the same thing as hinted-slope arithmetic: given P1, P2 and a claimed P3, the cheapest sound check is to hint the slope and verify the three identities above (2 M + 1 S); checking collinearity (y2 − y1)(x3 − x1) = (−y3 − y1)(x2 − x1) (2 M) plus on-curve of P3 (≈3 M) is more expensive. I know of no per-step relation below ≈ 3 field multiplications for an affine group operation, and there is no literature claim of one.
- Random-challenge checks cannot beat λ ≈ 128 doublings for one variable-base multiplication at 2^{−128} soundness (the Di Crescenzo bound is 2λ multiplications only because the bases are fixed and precomputed offline; Wesolowski-style checks cost two ℓ-bit exponentiations). The lattice approach is strictly better here because it is deterministic and reaches n^{1/4} = 64 bits.
- Pippenger/windowed partial sums with hints: the verifier must still check each hinted partial sum against its window, so the total addition count is unchanged and the doubling count stays equal to the longest scalar; the only benefit of windows is replacing adds by table lookups when lookups are cheaper than adds (which they are in our model if tables are of fixed points; for variable-point tables the table must be built with verified additions).
- ECIP verifier work in a native (non-R1CS) setting includes evaluating the committed function f (degree growing with the number of points and digits) at the challenge points and forming the digit-weighted sums; Garaga's measured ≈ 780 MulMod per point suggests this is roughly the cost of a 128-bit hinted double-and-add chain, so for 1–2 points ECIP is at best a wash. Its real strengths are (i) constant *multiplicative*-constraint count in R1CS where linear constraints are free, and (ii) better scaling for many points. For an ecrecover-per-transaction guest, (i) does not apply.
- The Personae T/U rewriting is only a win when T and U can be computed by someone whose cost is not counted; in our model computing T = r^{-1} R is itself a variable-base scalar multiplication, so it does not reduce cost; what carries over is the observation that with R known everything can be reduced to one variable-base multiplication plus fixed-base work, which the lattice method then halves/quarters.

### Gaps
- I could not read the full Eagen 2022 PDF (Cloudflare), so the exact verifier cost as a function of points and digits (base −3 decomposition, degree of f) is not quoted; only the Garaga implementation numbers and the Hall-Andersen–Aranha summary are.
- Garaga's MSM/ECDSA tests presumably include scalar decomposition and hint-consistency overheads; a breakdown of the 783 MulMod into ECIP core vs overhead was not available.

## Key question 4 — Known results on the complexity of verifying an EC scalar multiplication versus computing it (generic group model, delegation/outsourcing literature).

### Takeaway
No lower bound specifically for hint-assisted verification of k·P = Q in the generic group model turned up; the relevant known results are (a) Chevalier–Laguillaumie–Vergnaud (2016/Algorithmica): single-server *private* outsourcing with a constant number of generic group operations is impossible and their constructions are optimal, (b) Terelius–Wikström (SCN 2012): knowledge-error lower bounds for Σ-protocols for group homomorphisms in the GGM, (c) delegation protocols whose client cost is Θ(λ) group operations per 2^{−λ} soundness. Algebraic (non-generic) techniques such as ECIP evade generic-group counting, which is why no unconditional Ω(log n) bound can exist for algebraic verifiers.

### Cited Findings
- Chevalier–Laguillaumie–Vergnaud, "Privately Outsourcing Exponentiation to a Single Server: Cryptanalysis and Optimal Constructions" (ePrint 2016/309, Algorithmica): practical attacks on the ESORICS 2014 protocol; "our attacks on the ESORICS 2014 protocol are unavoidable" when the device is limited "to a constant number of (generic) group operations"; constructions for public/secret bases and exponents; "we show that our constructions are actually optimal" — [ePrint 2016/309](https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/309).
- Terelius–Wikström, efficiency limitations of Σ-protocols for group homomorphisms: within the GGM "the existing Σφ-protocol is optimal"; they generalize Shoup's result to arbitrary exponents and prove lower bounds on the knowledge error of exponentiation — [TW12 PDF](https://www.csc.kth.se/~dog/research/papers/TW12Conf.pdf).
- Di Crescenzo et al.: client cost 2λ + m + 4 modular multiplications for soundness 2^{−λ} (fixed bases, offline phase allowed); Okamoto verification "normally requiring … 3 exponentiations with 2048-bit exponents can now be delegated by a client that only uses 1 exponentiation to a random and short" exponent — [JMC 2021](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/151366/14/jmc_2019_0036_final.pdf).
- Hohenberger–Lysyanskaya (2005) introduced the formal outsourcing model with two servers; Di Crescenzo et al. survey the line (single-server fixed-base delegation, group inverses) — [JMC 2021 introduction](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/151366/14/jmc_2019_0036_final.pdf). Verifiable outsourcing of pairings is a related but separate line (Mefenza–Vergnaud) — [ENS PDF](https://www.di.ens.fr/~mefenza/Verifiable%20Outsourcing%20of%20Pairing%20Computations.pdf).
- Bitcoin-era measurement: key recovery costs about the same as verification (≈ 685 µs vs 650 µs, 2011 benchmark) — [bitcointalk topic 6430](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=6430).

### Inferences
- In the pure generic group model with hints that are group elements, a verifier can only relate labels through group operations, so establishing Q = kP given only P needs an addition chain connecting them; the lattice trick shows the right measure is not log k but the shortest vector in the lattice of relations the verifier is willing to check (n^{1/2} per extra variable base, n^{1/4} with a free endomorphism). I found no theorem formalizing a matching lower bound (e.g. "Ω(n^{1/4}) generic operations for verifying a 4-base relation") and none should be expected in the algebraic setting because ECIP replaces group operations by function-field evaluations.
- Practical conclusion: the best known verifier cost for one ecrecover on secp256k1 is Θ(n^{1/4}) ≈ 64–73 group-operation pairs on variable bases plus fixed-base work; anything lower would require a new algebraic technique (ECIP-type) that is cheaper than Garaga's ≈ 780 M per point in native arithmetic.

### Gaps
- No GGM lower bound for hinted verification of scalar multiplication was found; the question of whether 64-bit scalars are optimal for a verifier limited to generic operations plus the endomorphism is, as far as I can tell, open or at least unpublished.
- I did not access the full text of ePrint 2016/309 or TW12 to extract exact theorem statements beyond the abstracts.

## Key question 5 — Pairings or other bilinear structure for checking the result; curve cycles / secq256k1.

### Takeaway
secp256k1 has no usable pairing (embedding degree astronomically large), so no bilinear check of s R = z G + r Q exists on the curve itself; its cycle partner secq256k1 (base field = F_n) makes ECDSA's scalar-field arithmetic native inside a Spartan-style proof but is itself not pairing-friendly, and pairing-friendly cycles are essentially restricted to MNT families. A Cocks–Pinch curve with scalar field equal to secp256k1's base field would only make the group operations native in a pairing-based SNARK (as CP6-782 does for BLS12-377 and the Yafa curves for ed25519); it does not reduce the number of group operations the proof must cover.

### Cited Findings
- secp256k1 is listed as not vulnerable to multiplicative (MOV/Frey–Rück) transfer, i.e. its embedding degree is far too large for any pairing — [SafeCurves: transfers](https://safecurves.cr.yp.to/transfer.html).
- Spartan-ecdsa uses the secp256k1/secq256k1 cycle ("Secp256k1's scalar field is secq256k1's base field") so that "field arithmetic (for the signature) [can] be included in the constraints efficiently" (3,039 constraints for efficient-ECDSA) — [spartan-ecdsa README mirror](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fpersonaelabs%2Fspartan-ecdsa).
- Chiesa–Chua–Weidner, "On cycles of pairing-friendly elliptic curves" (arXiv 1803.02067, 2018): characterize all cycles of MNT curves, construct a length-4 MNT cycle, rule out 2-cycles for particular small embedding degrees, show "long cycles cannot be constructed from families of curves with the same complex multiplication discriminant", that cycles of composite-order curves cannot exist, and that no cycles consist solely of Freeman or Barreto–Naehrig curves — [arXiv 1803.02067](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.02067).
- Cocks–Pinch curves with a prescribed scalar field: CP6-782 "was generated using the Cocks–Pinch method for the embedding degree 6. The main feature of this curve is that the scalar field equals the base field of the BLS12_377 curve" — [ark-cp6-782](https://lib.rs/crates/ark-cp6-782); Yafa-108/146 embed ed25519 via "the standard recipe of the Cocks–Pinch method" — [ePrint 2022/1145](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1145). The Cocks–Pinch method inherently gives ρ ≈ 2 (base field ≈ twice the subgroup size) — [Heuristics of the Cocks–Pinch method](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1211.0971).

### Inferences
- A pairing check would need e(R, g2)^s = e(G, g2)^z · e(Q, g2)^r on a curve where R, G, Q live; since secp256k1 admits no pairing, this is impossible, and embedding secp256k1 points into another curve is not a group homomorphism, so there is no workaround that preserves the ECDSA relation.
- A hypothetical Cocks–Pinch curve with r = p_secp256k1 (not found in the literature I searched; speculative) would give a Groth16/PLONK-style system in which secp256k1 base-field arithmetic is native, i.e. the same benefit secq256k1 gives Spartan; the count of hinted group operations inside the proof is unchanged, so it is orthogonal to the formulation question.

### Gaps
- No published pairing-friendly curve whose scalar field is secp256k1's base field was found; the exact embedding degree value for secp256k1 is not quoted from a primary source (SafeCurves only states the qualitative result).

## Key question 6 — When only the Keccak address is needed: does witnessing Q and proving keccak(Q) = address plus signature validity change the cost?

### Takeaway
No cost change on the hash side: both recovery and witnessed-Q verification produce the affine key (x, y) and the address requires keccak256(x ‖ y) regardless; the only effect of witnessing Q is to enable the small-scalar verification equations (Q1), plus a ≈3 M on-curve check. Published circuit numbers show Keccak roughly doubling an ECDSA circuit in circom (163,239 → 315,175 constraints); Spartan-ecdsa avoided Keccak by hashing the key with Poseidon, which is not an option when the Ethereum address is the required output.

### Cited Findings
- Personae: `ecdsa_verify` 163,239 constraints; `ecdsa_verify_pubkey_to_eth_addr` (adds Keccak of the public key) 315,175 constraints; the post notes that if every address in a group has sent a transaction "we can extract the address's public key from the ECDSA signature of the transaction" and use the cheaper circuit, otherwise the Keccak-based circuit is needed — [Personae efficient-ECDSA post](https://digital-garden-bpz.pages.dev/blog/efficient-ecdsa).
- Spartan-ecdsa: 3,039 constraints for efficient ECDSA and "5,037 constraints for a depth 20 merkle tree membership check + 1 Poseidon hash of the ECDSA public key" (no Keccak) — [spartan-ecdsa README mirror](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fpersonaelabs%2Fspartan-ecdsa).
- Recovery output is the point Q itself (SEC 1 §4.1.6: Q = r^{-1}(sR − eG)) — [arXiv 2006.16714 citing SEC 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.16714); zkVMs expose secp256k1 add/double/decompress as precompiles and pair them with a Keccak precompile for ecrecover-heavy workloads — [SP1 precompiles docs](https://docs.succinct.xyz/docs/writing-programs/precompiles).

### Inferences
- Witnessing Q costs two hinted field elements (x_Q, y_Q) plus an on-curve check; the Keccak input is identical to the recovery case. There is no algebraic shortcut from (r, s, z, v) to the address that bypasses Q, because the address is a hash of the full affine key.
- If the guest architecture can delegate Keccak cheaply (as in this repo's keccak delegation), the ECDSA part dominates and the 4D formulation (Q1) is where the savings are; if Keccak is expensive (circom-style), it is comparable to the whole ECDSA check and neither formulation changes that.

### Gaps
- No source compares "recover-then-hash" vs "witness-then-verify-and-hash" directly in a native hint-model cost unit; the conclusion that the hash cost is identical is my inference from the fact that both produce the same Q.
