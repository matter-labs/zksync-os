# Circuit-level ECDSA secp256k1 verification / ecrecover gadgets: designs, non-native arithmetic, and costs

Scope: arithmetic circuits and STARK/SNARK gadgets (not plain RISC-V guest code) that prove secp256k1 ECDSA verification or public-key recovery, plus the dedicated "precompile" circuits that zkVMs attach for this purpose. Numbers are quoted with the year/version of the source where the source states one. Research date: 2026-10-29.

A note on units: the designs below report costs in incomparable units (R1CS constraints, halo2 advice cells, UltraHonk gates, PIL rows, Cairo steps, zkVM cycles + delegation calls). I keep each design's native unit and only convert where the conversion is mechanical and flagged as an inference.

## Key question 1: Reported constraint counts / rows / proving time per ECDSA verification or ecrecover, per design

### Takeaway
The spread is about three orders of magnitude: ~1.5M R1CS constraints for the 2022 circom-ecdsa baseline, ~160k when the two scalar multiplications are mostly moved out of the circuit (efficient-zk-ecdsa), ~3k when the circuit is built over the "right" field (Spartan-ecdsa on secq256k1), ~43k UltraHonk gates in Barretenberg's ACIR black box, ~0.5M halo2 advice cells (one 2^19 column) in halo2-lib/Axiom and Scroll, ~17k Arithmetic-SM rows + ~6.3k main-SM steps in Polygon zkEVM, ~153k Cairo steps for the two scalar multiplications of a Starknet recovery, and ~75k RISC-V cycles + ~780 precompile syscalls for one ecrecover in SP1 (third-party measurement, 2026).

### Cited Findings

**circom-ecdsa (0xPARC, Groth16/snarkjs, circom >= 2.0.2; built during 0xPARC's first Applied ZK learning group, 2022)**
- Constraint counts: `pubkeygen` 95,444; `eth_addr` 247,380; `groupsig` 250,938; `verify` 1,508,136. On a 16-core 3.0 GHz / 32 GB AWS c5.4xlarge: `verify` proving time 45 s, witness generation 175 s, proving key 934 MB; building `verify` needs 56 GB RAM. 256-bit values are represented as k = 4 registers of n = 64 bits, and the README credits an xJsnark optimization for big-integer multiplication — [0xPARC/circom-ecdsa README](https://github.com/0xPARC/circom-ecdsa)
- The baseline circuit for one verification comprised 2 BigMultModP, an ECDSAPrivToPub, a Secp256k1ScalarMult and a Secp256k1AddUnequal, totalling 1,508,136 constraints; original method took ~5 min of browser proving on a MacBook with ~1 GB of proof metadata — [Personae "Efficient ECDSA" post, Nov 30 2022 (mirror of personaelabs.org/posts/efficient-ecdsa-1)](https://digital-garden-bpz.pages.dev/blog/efficient-ecdsa)

**Polygon zkEVM (Hermez; zkASM ROM + Arithmetic state machine, PIL)**
- `ecrecover.zkasm` header counters: success path with square root: 528 arith, 523 binary, 6,294 steps, 1 keccak; no-square-root failure path: 1,014 arith, 10 binary, 4,527 steps; early-failure checks: 2 arith, 8 binary, 45 steps. ### Inferences
- halo2-lib's single-ECDSA circuit at k=19 with one advice column implies ≈ 2^19 ≈ 524k advice cells per verification; Scroll's 468,591 cells/sig with the same chip (3×88-bit limbs) is consistent. At ~4 cells per halo2-lib basic gate, this is ≈ 120k "gates" per ECDSA, i.e. ~3x Barretenberg's 42,838 UltraHonk gates, which use wider custom bigfield gates.
- Polygon's ecrecover cost is dominated by the Arithmetic SM (528 ops × 32 rows ≈ 17k rows) and the main-SM steps (~6.3k); the ROM comments show the scalar multiplication alone accounts for 512 of the arith ops in the worst case.
- Starknet's secp256k1 scalar multiplication (76.5k steps) is ~190x costlier than a point addition (406 steps), consistent with a bit-serial double-and-add of 256 hinted-slope steps.
- The SP1 third-party figure (783 syscalls per ecrecover) is consistent with a double-and-add/windowed scalar multiplication where each add/double is one syscall plus a few hundred uint256 mulmod calls.

### Gaps
- No official SP1 or OpenVM publication gives trace rows / cells per ecrecover; the 74,515-cycle figure is from a third-party PR and not independently verified.
- RISC Zero's `bigint2` circuit internals (rows per EC add/double, "program" encoding) could not be fetched (the circuit README path returned 404); no official per-ecrecover cycle count was found.
- No Plonky2 ECDSA gate count or proving time was found in any primary source.
- Polygon's PIL `arith.pil` was only read for the first 100k characters; the exact per-limb carry handling for secp256k1 EC equations beyond EQ1–EQ4 was not extracted.
- Barretenberg's exact gate breakdown of the 42,838-gate ECDSA (how many bigfield muls, biggroup window size) was not found; the bigfield README does not give a per-multiplication total.

## Key question 2: Which designs use the "right" field (cycle / amicable curve, e.g. secq256k1), and what does it cost/save?

### Takeaway
Only Spartan-ecdsa (Personae, 2023) proves secp256k1 ECDSA over secq256k1 so that base-field arithmetic is native; it cuts the ECDSA verification to 3,039 R1CS constraints versus ~160k in circom and documents that the "wrong field" portion alone would cost 11,494 constraints. Aztec's Goblin/ECCVM uses the same idea (Grumpkin proves BN254 ops) but only for BN254 recursion, not secp256k1; nobody else surveyed proves secp256k1 natively.

### Cited Findings
- spartan-ecdsa: "the fastest in-browser verification of ECDSA signatures in ZK, using Spartan on the secq256k1 curve"; the README attributes the drop from ~1.5M (circom-ecdsa) to ~3k constraints to right-field arithmetic over secq and to avoiding range checks and big-integer math; it requires a Circom build with secq256k1 support (`circom-secq`); hashing (Poseidon), not ECC, is now the bottleneck — [spartan-ecdsa README](https://github.com/personaelabs/spartan-ecdsa)
- Cost split in the same README: efficient ECDSA saves 14,505 constraints over standard verification: 11,494 for wrong-field (scalar-field) math to compute `s^-1`, `r·s^-1`, `m·s^-1` plus 3,011 for an extra `Secp256k1Mul` — [spartan-ecdsa README](https://github.com/personaelabs/spartan-ecdsa)
- The secp256k1/secq256k1 pair forms a cycle: one curve's scalar field is the other's base field, which matters because ECDSA verification touches both fields — [CSDN translation of the Spartan-ecdsa post](https://blog.csdn.net/mutourend/article/details/128886557)
- Personae's earlier post names the problem "wrong field arithmetic": operations over the secp256k1 base field differ from the scalar field of the curves snarkjs uses — [Personae post](https://digital-garden-bpz.pages.dev/blog/efficient-ecdsa)
- Aztec ECCVM: "proves correct execution of accumulated elliptic curve operations on BN-254" and proofs are made with Grumpkin, whose scalar field is BN254's base field F_q; secp256k1 is not mentioned — [ECCVM README](https://barretenberg.aztec.network/api/md_src_2barretenberg_2eccvm_2_r_e_a_d_m_e). Goblin Plonk defers BN254 group operations only; "other instructions could be added to the IM later, such as SHA256 or non-native field arithmetic" — [Goblin Plonk HackMD](https://hackmd.io/@aztec-network/B19AA8812)
- Goblin cost estimate (Aztec, "conservative"): each deferred non-native scalar multiplication costs 5 non-native field multiplications (~10 width-4 UltraPlonk-equivalent constraints each) plus 1 native scalar multiplication (~64 constraints) ≈ 114 constraints; 64 scalar multiplications ≈ 7,296 constraints versus "close to 300,000–400,000" brute-force UltraPlonk constraints — [Goblin Plonk HackMD](https://hackmd.io/@aztec-network/B19AA8812)
- ECCVM row costs: scalars are split into two 128-bit halves via the BN254 endomorphism; Straus with 4-bit digits (32 digits + skew), precomputed odd multiples ±1P..±15P and 2P stored in 8 rows per short scalar; 4 additions per VM row; MSM table for an m-term MSM ≈ (32+1)·⌈m/4⌉ + 31 rows ≈ 8.25 rows per short scalar — [ECCVM README](https://barretenberg.aztec.network/api/md_src_2barretenberg_2eccvm_2_r_e_a_d_m_e)
- Barretenberg's `ECCVM_RECURSIVE_VERIFIER_GATE_COUNT = 238260` and `GOBLIN_AVM_GATE_COUNT = 2950641` quantify the fixed overhead of the Goblin/ECCVM stack — [gate_count_constants.hpp](https://barretenberg.aztec.network/api/gate__count__constants_8hpp)
- a16z on Jolt: a curve-based zkVM "can avoid non-native field arithmetic that adds hundred-to-thousand-fold overheads to proving time"; "Jolt-with-curves" is recommended for EC statements — [a16z crypto](https://www.a16zcrypto.com/posts/article/understanding-jolt-clarifications-and-reflections)

### Inferences
- Right-field proving removes the limb decomposition entirely (3,039 vs 163,239 constraints: ~54x), but costs a proof system without pairings (Spartan/Hyrax over secq256k1), 16 kB proofs, and no cheap on-chain verification; it also forces the scalar-field work (s^-1 etc.) out of the circuit, which is why Personae's "efficient ECDSA" rearrangement is a prerequisite.
- The Goblin/ECCVM approach is a pattern (defer EC ops to a VM over the cycle curve, bridge with a translator using non-native field arithmetic) that would transfer to secp256k1 via secq256k1, but no implementation of that transfer was found.

### Gaps
- No design was found that proves secp256k1 ECDSA over secq256k1 inside a pairing-based or FRI-based system with an on-chain-friendly verifier; the "cost" side of a two-curve (secq256k1 + outer) stack is undocumented.
- The original Spartan-ecdsa blog post (personaelabs.org/posts/spartan-ecdsa) is offline; its proving-time comparison table could only be partially recovered from a translation.

## Key question 3: Which designs hint (witness) inverses, quotients, carries or the whole scalar-multiplication trace, and how large are the checks?

### Takeaway
Every design surveyed hints at least the quotient of each non-native multiplication; most also hint field inverses/slopes (checked by one multiplication), square roots (checked by one squaring plus a parity check), and point-operation results (checked via slope identities). The size of the per-step check ranges from 1 native multiplication gate + a few range checks (Barretenberg prime-limb check) to 32 PIL rows (Polygon) or ~24 Cairo steps per scalar bit.

### Cited Findings
- halo2-lib ecdsa: `u1 = msghash·s^-1`, `u2 = r·s^-1` are computed with `divide_unsafe` (sound only if s ≠ 0, checked separately via `is_soft_nonzero` on r, s in [1, n−1]); `big_less_than` enforces u1, u2 < n; `fixed_base::scalar_multiply` for u1·G and variable-base `scalar_multiply` for u2·Q with `fixed_window_bits` / `var_window_bits`; sum via `chip.sum` handling equal points; x-coordinate compared to r as integers after constraining x < n ("Only valid when p is very close to n in size (e.g. for Secp256k1)"; only use when 1/(p−n) < ~2^-100); pubkey must not be identity (not checked) — [halo2-ecc ecdsa.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/axiom-crypto/halo2-lib/main/halo2-ecc/src/ecc/ecdsa.rs)
- Scroll: recovery id handled by witnessing `tmp` with `y_low_limb = v + 2·tmp`, `tmp` range-checked to 87 bits; pubkey checked `is_on_curve_or_infinity`; keccak(pubkey) checked through a keccak lookup — [sig_circuit.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scroll-tech/zkevm-circuits/develop/zkevm-circuits/src/sig_circuit.rs)
- Barretenberg bigfield: quotient q and remainder r are computed natively and checked by (C1) `a·b − q·p − r ≡ 0 mod 2^272` via schoolbook limb products split into two 136-bit halves with range-constrained carries, (C2) one native multiplication gate on the prime limbs `a'·b' + q'·(n−p) − r' = 0`, and (C3) the CRT bound `a·b < M` and `q·p + r < M` with `M = 2^272·n`; division/inversion computes `c = a·b^-1` natively and checks `b·c = a mod p` with an offset `u·p` and `b ≠ 0`; q is constrained to one limb (< 2^68); remainder is lazily reduced to `r < 2^|p|` — [bigfield README](https://barretenberg.aztec.network/api/md_src_2barretenberg_2stdlib_2primitives_2bigfield_2_r_e_a_d_m_e). zkSecurity audit (July 1 2024): quotient is a prover-supplied witness (`create_from_u512_as_witness`, sized by `num_quotient_bits`); `self_reduce()` multiplies by 1 using a witnessed quotient and replaces the value by the witnessed remainder; carries range-checked to ~70 bits; Ultra builder caches partial products (`cached_partial_non_native_field_multiplication` with lo0/hi0/hi1 witnesses) — [zkSecurity Aztec bigfield audit](https://reports.zksecurity.xyz/reports/aztec-bigfield/)
- Polygon zkEVM: inverse of r via `invFnEc` "using a hint"; y recovered via `sqrtFpEc` which supplies a hinted root plus a flag; if no root exists `checkSqrtFpEc` verifies non-existence (a failed check is treated as a malicious prover, error 6); root parity must match v; k1, k2 are range-checked "to rule out aliased values"; header: "MAP: MAlicious Prover" — hints treated as untrusted — [ecrecover.zkasm](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/0xPolygonHermez/zkevm-rom/main/main/ecrecover/ecrecover.zkasm). In `mulPointEc`, "the prover computes the x and y coordinates of each sum or double off-circuit, as hints. The ARITH opcode then checks the elliptic-curve relation" (`ARITH_ECADD_DIFFERENT`, `ARITH_ECADD_SAME`); point-at-infinity is tracked by flags — [mulPointEc.zkasm](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/0xPolygonHermez/zkevm-rom/main/main/ecrecover/mulPointEc.zkasm). The arith check itself is a 32-row operation with equations `s·x2 − s·x1 − y2 + y1 + q0·p = 0`, `2·s·y1 − 3·x1² + q0·p = 0`, `s² − x1 − x2 − x3 + q1·p = 0`, `s·x1 − s·x3 − y1 − y3 + q2·p = 0`, with per-chunk inverses (`xDeltaChunkInverse`) proving x1 ≠ x2 and `chunkLtPrime`/`valueLtPrime` to keep x3, y3 in [0, p−1] — [arith.pil](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/0xPolygonHermez/zkevm-proverjs/main/pil/arith.pil)
- Cairo (cairo-lang `cairo_secp`): `div_mod_n(a, b)` gets `res` and quotient `k` from a hint and checks `res·b = k·N + a` limb by limb with range-checked carries; `get_point_from_x` hints `y = (x³+β)^((P+1)/4)` with parity chosen by v and checks `y² = x³ + β`; `try_get_point_from_x` hints `is_on_curve` and checks `y² = −(x³+β)` in the false branch; limb bounds: prover ≈ ±2^88 per limb, soundness bound ≈ ±2^107; `validate_signature_entry` checks r, s in [1, N) limb-wise — [signature.cairo](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/starkware-libs/cairo-lang/master/src/starkware/cairo/common/cairo_secp/signature.cairo). `ec_mul` splits the scalar into 86/86/84-bit limbs and `ec_mul_inner` processes one hinted bit per recursion (double, then conditional `fast_ec_add`); slopes come from hints (`compute_slope`, `compute_doubling_slope`) and are checked by `(x0−x1)·slope − y0 + y1 = 0` / `3x² − 2·slope·y = 0`; new coordinates are hinted and checked with `slope² − new_x − x0 − x1` etc. via `unreduced_mul` + `verify_zero` — [ec.cairo](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/starkware-libs/cairo-lang/master/src/starkware/cairo/common/cairo_secp/ec.cairo)
- gnark emulated: "when an element is computed using hints, every limb must not be wider than k bits", so hint outputs are bit-decomposed and checked; reduction proves `x' = k·q + x` with k and x' from hints; equality check is `diff · 1 = 0 + k · r` — [gnark v0.10 emulated doc.go](https://github.com/consensys/gnark/blob/v0.10.0/std/math/emulated/doc.go), [ivokub HackMD](https://hackmd.io/@ivokub/SyJRV7ye2)
- plonky2-ecdsa: `add_virtual_nonnative_target`/`add_virtual_affine_point_target` create prover-supplied targets later connected to computed values; `inv_nonnative` in `curve_add`/`curve_double` — [curve.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/0xPolygonZero/plonky2-ecdsa/main/src/gadgets/curve.rs)
- Airbender: host-provided hints for inverses are checked by one multiplication (`a * a⁻¹ == 1`); the example `examples/ecrecover-hooks/` and `crates/airbender-crypto/tests/secp256k1.rs` are the reference — [airbender crypto guide](https://matter-labs.github.io/airbender-platform/latest/04-crypto-on-guest-and-host.html). l2iterative's RISC Zero implementation hints the inverse of r, the square root (also to prove Legendre-symbol non-residuosity) and the slopes of every point operation, so the guest never divides; it computes `a·b // n` with two modular multiplications (mod n and mod n−1, proved in Coq) — [l2iterative/secp256k10](https://github.com/l2iterative/secp256k10)
- xJsnark (2018): the product z of two m-limb integers is provided as a witness and verified by checking `(Σ x[i]c^i)(Σ y[i]c^i) = Σ z[i]c^i` for c = 1..2m−1, i.e. 2m−1 constraints, exploiting that multiplication by constants is free — [xJsnark PDF, §4.B.1](https://akosba.github.io/papers/xjsnark.pdf)
- "Casting out Primes" (Lubarov & Baylina, ePrint 2022/1470, Oct 2022): nondeterministic bignum arithmetic verified by checking an identity f(x) = 0 modulo each modulus in a set M; if |f(x)| < lcm(M) the identity holds over the integers — [ePrint 2022/1470](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1470)

### Inferences
- The size of a hinted point operation check is roughly constant across designs: 3 non-native multiplications (slope check, x3 check, y3 check) plus range checks. In Polygon this is one 32-row arith op per add/double; in halo2-lib/Barretenberg it is ~3 bigfield muls (each ~3 custom gates in Barretenberg); in Cairo ~24 steps per bit including bookkeeping.
- "Full-trace hinting" (prover supplies every intermediate point) is universal in the STARK/VM designs (Polygon, Cairo, zkVM precompiles); SNARK gadget libraries instead hint only inverses/quotients and compute the rest, because computing a sum from the slope costs no more than checking it.

### Gaps
- No source quantified the exact per-ECDSA count of hinted values (number of inversions/sqrt) for halo2-lib or Barretenberg.
- The `verify_zero` implementation in cairo-lang (how the quotient is bounded) was not read.

## Key question 4: Cost of non-native 256-bit field multiplication in circuits and lookup-based tricks

### Takeaway
All production designs use CRT-style checking (mod the native field plus mod 2^T via limbs) with lookup-table range checks on limbs and carries; the dominant layouts are 3×88-bit limbs (halo2-lib, Scroll, PSE), 4×68-bit limbs (Barretenberg, T = 272), 3×86-bit (Cairo), 4×64-bit (circom-ecdsa) and 16×16-bit (Polygon PIL). Barretenberg packs a 256-bit modular multiplication into about 3 custom gates plus range checks; halo2-lib needs a 2^19-row advice column for a whole ECDSA; Polygon spends 32 rows per 256-bit multiply-add.

### Cited Findings
- Barretenberg bigfield: 4 limbs of L = 68 bits (T = 272) plus a "prime limb" holding the value mod the native modulus; a custom bigfield multiplication gate performs 4 and 3 witness-witness multiplications in two gate types, "three such gates cover the multiplication"; a naive Plonk gate (one multiplication each) "would need at least 10 gates before extra additions"; addition/subtraction 4 gates each; limbs may grow to 86 bits before reduction; `pow()` example 8,748 → 6,455 gates after an audit fix — [bigfield README](https://barretenberg.aztec.network/api/md_src_2barretenberg_2stdlib_2primitives_2bigfield_2_r_e_a_d_m_e), [zkSecurity audit](https://reports.zksecurity.xyz/reports/aztec-bigfield/). Range checks use plookup helpers (`decompose_non_native_field_double_width_limb` on plookup builders, `decompose_into_default_range` for carries) — [zkSecurity audit](https://reports.zksecurity.xyz/reports/aztec-bigfield/). The Zellic report describes the queued data as "a non-native field multiplication identity a * b = q * p + r" and found a critical overflow in `evaluate_non_native_field_multiplication` — [Zellic report (search snippet)](https://reports.zellic.io/publications/barretenberg-bigfield/findings/critical-ultracircuitbuilder-overflow-possible-in-evaluate-non-native-field-multiplication)
- halo2-lib: 3 limbs × 88 bits, range checks by lookup into a `2^lookup_bits` table with `lookup_bits = k − 1`, requiring dedicated "lookup advice" columns (1–53 in the ECDSA benchmark) — [bench_ecdsa.config](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/axiom-crypto/halo2-lib/main/halo2-ecc/configs/secp256k1/bench_ecdsa.config), [halo2-lib README](https://github.com/axiom-crypto/halo2-lib). Scroll measured 468,591 advice cells + 64,227 lookup cells per ECDSA with this chip — [utils.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/scroll-tech/zkevm-circuits/develop/zkevm-circuits/src/sig_circuit/utils.rs)
- circom-ecdsa: 4×64-bit registers; big-integer multiplication uses the xJsnark polynomial-identity trick (2m−1 constraints per m-limb product instead of m²) — [circom-ecdsa README](https://github.com/0xPARC/circom-ecdsa), [xJsnark](https://akosba.github.io/papers/xjsnark.pdf). In R1CS the dominant cost is not the products but the bit-decomposition range checks: gnark notes a bit-decomposition range check costs ≈ 2·t constraints per multiplication (t = native field bit length) — [ivokub HackMD](https://hackmd.io/@ivokub/SyJRV7ye2)
- gnark emulated (Groth16/PLONK, BN254 native): limb-wise multiplication costs ~k² operations; global limb width w, per-element overflow tracking; proposed lookup-based range checks via a LegoSNARK-style commitment with a product or log-derivative (Haböck) argument — cost ≈ b·log N + N multiplications, b-constraint fixed gadget overhead; measured BN254-in-BN254 pairing 1,393,318 constraints / 5.7 s and BLS12-381-in-BN254 2,088,277 / 7.4 s on an M1 — [ivokub HackMD](https://hackmd.io/@ivokub/SyJRV7ye2), [gnark emulated docs](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/consensys/gnark@v0.10.0/std/math/emulated), [zkStudyClub slides citing ePrint 2022/1470](https://www.slideshare.net/AlexPruden/zkstudyclub-improving-performance-of-nonnative-arithmetic-in-snarks-ivo-kubjas-consensys-gnark); zkSecurity audited gnark std's non-native arithmetic for Linea (May 2024) — [zkSecurity gnark audit](https://reports.zksecurity.xyz/reports/consensys-gnark-std/)
- Polygon zkEVM Arithmetic SM: 256-bit operands as 16 limbs of 16 bits; one op = 32 clocks; the base equation is `x1·y1 + x2 = y2·2^256 + y3` (EQ0); secp256k1 add/double (EQ1–EQ4), BN254 Fp2 mul/add/sub (EQ5–EQ10) and secp256r1 add/double (EQ11–EQ14) share the machine; limbs and quotients range-checked by lookups into `BYTE2` / `BYTE2_BIT21` tables — [arith.pil](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/0xPolygonHermez/zkevm-proverjs/main/pil/arith.pil); docs describe the Arithmetic SM as handling point add/double plus 256-bit add/mul/div — [Polygon docs (search snippet)](https://docs.polygon.technology/zkEVM/architecture/zkprover/arithmetic-sm)
- Cairo: BigInt3 with 86-bit limbs (`ec_mul` splits scalars 86/86/84); a `secp256k1_mul` consumes 7,045 range-check builtin uses, `secp256k1_add` 29 — [ec.cairo](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/starkware-libs/cairo-lang/master/src/starkware/cairo/common/cairo_secp/ec.cairo), [blockifier constants](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/starkware-libs/sequencer/main/crates/blockifier/resources/blockifier_versioned_constants_0_13_1.json)
- Goblin estimate: one non-native field multiplication ≈ 10 width-4 UltraPlonk-equivalent constraints; one native scalar multiplication ≈ 64 — [Goblin Plonk HackMD](https://hackmd.io/@aztec-network/B19AA8812)
- Thakur & Shenvi describe a protocol for multiple univariate polynomial products in a circuit where "everything except range checks, which are handled by a lookup argument, [is] relatively cheap" — [HackMD](https://hackmd.io/@3WHkK2CBQrKfvT5B6u-pog/Skv-OoUpT)
- RISC Zero's original `bigint` accelerator: ~10 cycles per 256-bit modular multiplication — [RISC Zero optimization guide (mirror)](https://www.mintlify.com/risc0/risc0/advanced/optimization)

### Inferences
- With 3×88-bit limbs a schoolbook product has 9 limb products folded into 5 columns; the remaining cost is dominated by range-checking q, r and 2–3 carries (each 88-bit limb = ~5 lookups at 18 bits), which is why halo2-lib's ECDSA needs ~64k lookup cells per signature alongside 469k arithmetic cells (~12%).
- Barretenberg's advantage (42,838 gates per ECDSA vs ~120k halo2-lib gate-equivalents) comes mostly from a wider custom gate that evaluates the entire binary-basis identity in 3 gates and from plookup range decomposition, not from a different algebraic idea.
- 16-bit limbs (Polygon) trade more rows (32 per op) for carry-free products that fit a 64-bit Goldilocks field and a single 2^16 lookup table.

### Gaps
- Mina/Kimchi's foreign-field-multiplication gate (3×88-bit limbs with 12-bit lookup range checks) is a relevant lookup-centric design but its RFC could not be fetched (404 on both the book URL and raw GitHub path), so no numbers are recorded.
- No ePrint paper specifically on "lookup-based big integer multiplication" or "Barrett reduction in circuits" was found; the gnark log-derivative range-check note is the closest primary source. The "Casting out Primes" full text (403) would give the small-moduli cost model.
- OpenVM's algebra extension (limb size, range-tuple checker) could not be fetched (docs and raw paths 404, DeepWiki 429).

## Key question 5: "Verify instead of compute" and probabilistic / batched checking for scalar multiplication

### Takeaway
The dominant trick is algebraic rearrangement rather than randomization: Personae's `s·T + U = Q` moves both inverses and one full scalar multiplication outside the circuit for free (verifier recomputes T, U from public r, R, m), and precomputed window tables of T passed as public inputs replace the remaining doublings. Randomized checks exist only at the field-arithmetic layer (gnark's commit-API random evaluation point for product identities) and in classical randomized batch verification; no circuit design was found that verifies many ECDSA scalar multiplications sublinearly.

### Cited Findings
- Efficient ECDSA rearrangement: from `s·R = m·G + r·Q` derive `s·(r^-1 R) − (r^-1 m)·G = Q`; define `T = r^-1·R`, `U = −r^-1·m·G`; circuit checks `s·T + U = Q` with one `Secp256k1ScalarMult` and one `Secp256k1AddUnequal`; T and U are deterministic functions of public values so the prover cannot choose them; the verifier must be given R (not only r) since (r, −s) is also valid; 8-bit stride tables of T (8,192 points) as public inputs give 163,239 constraints — [Personae post](https://digital-garden-bpz.pages.dev/blog/efficient-ecdsa); originally proposed by Vivek (0xPARC/Personae) on Oct 5 2022; a second circuit was suggested to verify the precomputed multiples; passing `r^-1·m` and using multiples of G reduces inputs further — [ethresear.ch thread](https://ethresear.ch/t/efficient-ecdsa-signature-verification-using-circom/13629)
- Spartan-ecdsa keeps only `s·T + U == Q_a` plus Merkle/Poseidon in circuit; T and U are computed outside and "this does not break correctness" — [spartan-ecdsa README](https://github.com/personaelabs/spartan-ecdsa)
- Batch circom ECDSA amortizes to ~0.48M constraints/signature at batch 32 (from 1.5M); technique not documented in the README — [puma314/batch-ecdsa](https://github.com/puma314/batch-ecdsa)
- gnark proposal: check the limb-polynomial identity `p_y(Z) = p_k(Z)·p_q(Z)` at a random point derived in-circuit from a Pedersen commitment to witness elements (LegoSNARK-style, with a proof of knowledge), replacing per-point evaluation; the same commitment supplies the challenge for a log-derivative lookup range check — [ivokub HackMD](https://hackmd.io/@ivokub/SyJRV7ye2)
- Goblin Plonk: instead of computing non-native group operations in-circuit, write them to a committed instruction transcript and prove the transcript in a native-field ECCVM; the translator checks consistency by evaluating the Grumpkin polynomials at a random challenge inside a BN254 circuit — [Goblin Plonk HackMD](https://hackmd.io/@aztec-network/B19AA8812)
- Polygon zkEVM and Cairo hint every point and verify it (see Q3); Polygon's ROM treats a wrong hint as a malicious prover and fails the proof — [ecrecover.zkasm](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/0xPolygonHermez/zkevm-rom/main/main/ecrecover/ecrecover.zkasm)
- Randomized ECDSA batch verification (outside circuits): Karati et al. use randomizers, but "the computation of b scalar multiplications significantly reduce the performance gained by batching" — [SPACE 2014 paper](https://oldweb.isical.ac.in/~skarati/pub_pdf/SPACE-2014-ECDSA.pdf); ePrint 2026/663 proposes modified ECDSA variants with extra signature bits for batching (≈30.9% / 17.2% gain at batch 32 on secp256k1), i.e. not applicable to standard ECDSA — [ePrint 2026/663](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/663)
- halo2-lib's ECDSA skips the mod-n reduction of the x-coordinate and instead constrains x < n and compares to r as integers, accepting a 1/(p−n) ≈ 2^-128 completeness gap for secp256k1 — [halo2-ecc ecdsa.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/axiom-crypto/halo2-lib/main/halo2-ecc/src/ecc/ecdsa.rs)

### Inferences
- The `s·T + U = Q` rearrangement is directly applicable to ecrecover in a zkVM/STARK context: with R known from (r, v), the verifier/host can compute `T = r^-1 R` and `U = −r^-1 m G` and the proof only needs one variable-base multiplication plus one addition; the saving (one fixed-base scalar multiplication and all scalar-field inversions) was worth ~100k R1CS constraints in circom and 14.5k in Spartan.
- Randomized "verify many scalar multiplications at once" techniques appear only where the circuit has an in-circuit source of randomness (Fiat–Shamir over a commitment); in STARK/AIR settings this corresponds to multi-round challenges, which is how lookup/permutation arguments already work, but no source applies it to EC scalar multiplication.

### Gaps
- No primary source for a sublinear or randomized in-circuit batch check of ECDSA scalar multiplications over standard secp256k1 signatures was found.
- The Succinct batch-ECDSA blog post (which may describe shared-table or random-combination tricks) is offline.
- No measurement was found of the precomputed-table-as-public-input approach in a STARK/zkVM setting (where public-input cost differs from Groth16).
